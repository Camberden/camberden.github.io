 So I had my first week at work in this new position. I am afforded a spacious, solitary, solo office wherein I am free to focus on learning and applying new skills toward this new career. It's more than I deserve, I'd say, as per usual. It really is lovely, and I have a whole hour to enjoy the sunshine in the middle of the day. My adventure in this role so far had mostly been in the form of training and traveling for training; I set out for two other sites within the city of Raleigh and to a region office out in Greenville, NC. Though it's been adventurous, the pangs of dejection had weighed heavily upon me, mostly stemming from the aforementioned fallout, partially stemming from my long commutes and temporary income reduction in accepting this role.
  Many views of the city, many spots, streets, and fixtures in workspace proximity are triggering despondancy. Thoughts flood my mind as a deluge of regret. Though the sun welcomes my presence, it had set in my inner world. This new role brights new perspective, and it's helping me understand and grow. Working this kind of job leaves me craving for social connection moreso than all previous ones held. Time moves much more slowly. That is a good thing. Free time is generally more plentiful on the day-by-day. That also is a good thing. Yet, it presents a novel void in my life that is largely unfilled, that of enjoying a great deal of this time I achieved with community and close company. Concerning community, I am getting a solid start in routinizing my exercise with CrossFit training in the early mornings. I plan on reintegrating myself into the practice group either on Tuesday evenings or Sunday mornings. I should also consider groups local to Chapel Hill and inquire about volunteer opportunities. These are now achievable and my energy would be sufficient. Concerning close company, well, I lost them and I deserve it. I keep thinking to myself: "Oh how amazing it could have been to spend many breaktimes, evenings, and nights in the city with someone dear to me, to enjoy the weekends together and plan with synchronized schedules and copious energy! How I would have loved to initiate these changes, to shift my priorities to our wellbeing, and to bring intentionality to cultivating our lives in service to our betterment. I could have been the one to further brighten the days, could have been the reliable bedrock of support, and could have been the overall improvement to life that they had faith I could become." I was considered worth it; I held value to them in my potential despite my shortcomings and my situation. They held value to me in their beautiful humility, sense of peace and order, vivacious and exploratory nature, unwavering patience and care, lovely smile, admirable intelligence, and amazing lifestyle and healthy habits that I aspired to emulate soon enough. I had not reciprocated well. I had put forth such a lackluster effort. I let my graces and excuses of working hours, school, and incomplete hobby projects dominate my headspace. I let my fast-passing nights with long hours prevent me from seeing that their daytime schedule and work can surface equally genuine life struggles and goals. I was overconcerned with my own struggles and goals, and was too blind to see that I was chosen to be the one to help relieve their struggles as one of their goals. I understand, now, better. Yet, I've been hurting, because I allowed the faith placed in me to be the best for myself and for them - to be the highlight of the next and better chapter of their life - to be tarnished, eroded, and completely destroyed, and in so doing, I must live with the knowledge that a person dear to me who I much admired had suffered in large part because of my failures: I was a massive disappointment, a waste of time, a terrible investment, a cause of sadness, discomfort, and emotional pain, so much so to deserve having been discarded like trash on a random day. I don't often regard other's opinions of me very much, but for people such as the one whose care I betrayed, I certainly do, and it burns. From precious to trash, I went. I, the flammable trash, burn as a dumpster fire in a Shanghai alley. So appropriate! I was taken out alright, lol. Silliness aside, my spirit craves for a chance to repay and to make amends, somehow. It was the wrong time, I knew it, and I'm sorry to have accepted the advances. Time will be a remedy. At the least, I can finish that book and return it to its rightful place. How befitting, its title: The Courage to be Disliked.
  Onward to this next episode of new-career me, to learn more and to continue my struggle in sorting these through these thoughts and relinquishing this false characterization of self as trash and deserving of continued suffering. It'll be okay. I know it will, I just need to vent it out, and surely I'm not done yet, hah. Probably a few more entries. The headquarters shop where I'll pick up some shirts tomorrow is awfully close to where I'd frequent those many months. Some day soon I'll look at associated places and things fondly with a smile and sweet sentiment, but until then, I just need the courage to be disliked.
